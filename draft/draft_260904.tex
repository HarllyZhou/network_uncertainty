\section{Benchmark Model}\label{sec:benchmark_model}
\subsection{Model Setup}
\subsubsection{Sectors and Firms}

Consider a closed-economy with $N$ industries, indexed by 
$i=1,\cdots,N$. Each industry has a representative firm that produces a
differentiated good $y_{i,t}$ using capital $k_{i,t}$, labor $n_{i,t}$,
and intermediate inputs from industry $j$, 
$\{x_{ij,t}\}_{j=1}^{N}$. The production function is given by
\begin{align}\label{eq:prod_func}
    y_{i,t}
    =
    z_{i,t}\zeta_{i,t}
    \left(
        k_{i,t}^{\eta_i}n_{i,t}^{1-\eta_i}
    \right)^{a_i}
    \left[
        \prod_{j=1}^{N}
        \left(\gamma_{j,t}x_{ij,t}\right)^{\alpha_{ij}}
    \right]^{1-a_i},
\end{align}
where $z_{i,t}$ is the productivity of industry $i$,
$\zeta_{i,t}$ is a normalizing factor, and
$\gamma_{j,t}x_{ij,t}$ is the quantity of good $j$ actually delivered
to industry $i$. Assume that
$\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{ij}=1$.

Capital evolves according to
\begin{align}\label{eq:capital_accumulation}
    k_{i,t+1}
    =
    \xi_{i,t}
    \left[
        (1-\delta^K)k_{i,t}+i_{i,t}
    \right],
\end{align}
where $\delta^K$ is the physical capital depreciation rate,
$i_{i,t}$ is investment, and
$\xi_{i,t}\in[0,1]$ is the remaining proportion of renewed capital
after potential liquidation. For the benchmark, we assume that there
is no adjustment cost for investment.

The stochastic productivity process is
\begin{align}\label{eq:firm_productivity}
    \log z_{i,t}
    =
    \log\bar z_i-\frac{\sigma_{i,t}^2}{2}
    +\sigma_{i,t}\epsilon_{i,t}.
\end{align}
Here $\bar z_i$ is sector-specific average productivity,
$\sigma_{i,t}^2$ is the innovation variance, and $\epsilon_{i,t}$ is
the productivity innovation. The term $-\sigma_{i,t}^2/2$ is a
normalization to avoid the mean of productivity fluctuating with the
innovation variance. Let
\begin{align*}
    \boldsymbol\epsilon_t
    =
    (\epsilon_{1,t},\ldots,\epsilon_{N,t})',
    \qquad
    \boldsymbol\epsilon_t
    \sim
    \mathcal N(\mathbf 0, \mathbf{I_N}),
\end{align*}
where $\mathbf{I_N}$ is the $N\times N$ identity matrix. For each $i$, $\epsilon_{i,t}$ has mean zero and unit variance. It realizes together with productivity $z_{i,t}$.

Suppose that the innovation variance of each sector moves over time
according to a two-state Markov chain with values
$\sigma_{i,H}^2>\sigma_{i,L}^2$ for all $i$. The transition matrix is
\begin{align*}
    \mathbf Q_i
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        q_i^H & 1-q_i^H \\
        1-q_i^L & q_i^L
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{align*}
so that the probability of staying in the high state is $q_i^H$ and
the probability of staying in the low state is $q_i^L$.

At the beginning of each period $t$, the innovation variance realizes,
while the productivity innovation and thus productivity have not yet
realized. The firm decides the nominal price $p_{i,t}$ and borrowing
$b_{i,t}$ from households. Borrowing is used to finance labor costs,
investment costs, and the costs of delivered intermediate inputs.

After productivity realizes, the firm decides labor demand $n_{i,t}$,
intermediate input orders $x_{i1,t},\ldots,x_{iN,t}$, and investment
$i_{i,t}$. Households submit consumption orders
$\{c_{i,t}\}_{i=1}^{N}$. The actual quantities delivered are determined
by the proportional rationing rates $\{\gamma_{i,t}\}_{i=1}^{N}$.

Goods-market clearing in industry $i$ is
\begin{align}\label{eq:goods_market_clearing}
    (1-\delta^v)v_{i,t-1}+y_{i,t}
    =
    \gamma_{i,t}
    \underbrace{\left(
        c_{i,t}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{ji,t}
    \right)}_{\equiv y^d_{i,t}}
    +v_{i,t},
\end{align}
where $v_{i,t}$ is the end-of-period inventory of good $i$ and
$\delta^v$ is its depreciation rate. Inventories and proportional
rationing satisfy
\begin{align*}
    v_{i,t}\geq 0,
    \qquad
    0\leq\gamma_{i,t}\leq 1,
    \qquad
    v_{i,t}(1-\gamma_{i,t})=0.
\end{align*}
When supply exceeds desired demand, $\gamma_{i,t}=1$ and the remaining
goods become inventories. When desired demand exceeds supply,
$v_{i,t}=0$ and all purchasers receive the same proportion
$\gamma_{i,t}$ of their orders.

Actual sales of industry $i$ are $\gamma_{i,t} y^d_{i,t}$. Therefore, after sales are realized, the firm earns nominal revenue $p_{i,t} \gamma_{i,t} y^d_{i,t}$, subject to a proportional revenue tax $\tau_{i,t}$. Here we assume
that revenue is observable and that creditors have seniority over
revenue and pledgeable assets.

The firm repays borrowing $b_{i,t}$ at the interest rate
$r_t+s_{i,t}$, where $r_t$ is the risk-free nominal interest rate and $s_{i,t}$
is the sector-specific risk premium set by creditors to compensate
for default risk. Default occurs if the firm does not have sufficient
enforceable resources. If after-tax sales revenue is insufficient for
repayment, the firm liquidates some of the renewed capital
\[
    (1-\delta^K)k_{i,t}+i_{i,t}.
\]
For simplicity, assume that there is a maximum proportion of capital
liquidation, so that at least
$\underline{\xi}\in(0,1)$ of renewed capital is carried into the next
period. Therefore,
$\xi_{i,t}\in[\underline{\xi},1]$. This restriction abstracts from
firm entry and bankruptcy. If there is a positive remaining profit,
the firm distributes it to households.

In summary, firms make decisions in two stages. After the innovation
variances are realized but before productivity is realized, firm $i$
chooses its nominal price $p_{i,t}$, borrowing $b_{i,t}$, and
investment $i_{i,t}$. Its first-stage problem is
\begin{align}\label{eq:firm_first_stage_decision}
    J_{i,t}
    \left(
        \mathbf{k}_t,
        \mathbf{v}_{t-1},
        \boldsymbol{\sigma}_t
    \right)
    =
    \max_{p_{i,t},b_{i,t},i_{i,t}}
    \mathbb{E}_{i,t}
    \Bigg[
            d_{i,t}
            +
            \mathcal{M}_{t,t+1}
            J_{i,t+1}
            \left(
                \mathbf{k}_{t+1},
                \mathbf{v}_t,
                \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{t+1}
            \right)
    \Bigg].
\end{align}
Here $\mathbb{E}_{i,t}$ denotes the expectation conditional on the
information available after the innovation variances are realized
but before productivity is realized. $d_{i,t}$ denotes the state-contigent maximized profit of the firm after productivity is realized, defined by
\begin{align*}
    d_{i,t} = \max\left\{0, (1-\tau_{i,t})p_{i,t}\gamma_{i,t} y^d_{i,t} - (1+r_t+s_{i,t})\ell_{i,t}\right\}.
\end{align*}

In the second stage, the firm decides its demand for inputs to minimize the cost:
\begin{align}\label{eq:firm_second_stage_decision}
    \min_{n_{i,t}, \{x_{ij,t}\}_{j=1}^{N}}
    \left\{
        w_t n_{i,t} + \sum_{j=1}^{N} p_{j,t} x_{ij,t}
    \right\}
\end{align}
subject to
\begin{align*}
    w_t n_{i,t} + \sum_{j=1}^{N} p_{j,t} x_{ij,t} &\leq b_{i,t} - p_{K,t} i_{i,t} \\
    y_{i,t}
    &=
    z_{i,t}\zeta_{i,t}
    \left(
        k_{i,t}^{\eta_i}n_{i,t}^{1-\eta_i}
    \right)^{a_i}
    \left[
        \prod_{j=1}^{N}
        \left(\gamma_{j,t}x_{ij,t}\right)^{\alpha_{ij}}
    \right]^{1-a_i}.
\end{align*}
\subsubsection{Households}

There are representative households with unit mass supplying labor
and credit to firms and submitting consumption orders
$\{c_{i,t}\}_{i=1}^{N}$. Household utility is given by
\begin{align}\label{eq:hh_utility}
    U(C_t,N_t)
    =
    \log C_t
    -
    \chi\frac{N_t^{1+\varphi}}{1+\varphi},
\end{align}
where consumption is an aggregator of delivered consumption goods,
\begin{align*}
    C_t
    :=
    \left[
        \sum_{i=1}^{N}
        \left(\gamma_{i,t}c_{i,t}\right)^{
            \frac{\theta-1}{\theta}
        }
    \right]^{
        \frac{\theta}{\theta-1}
    },
\end{align*}
and
\[
    N_t=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i,t}
\]
is total labor supply.

The household budget constraint is
\begin{align}\label{eq:hh_budget_constraint}
    w_tN_t
    +\sum_{i=1}^{N}
        (1+r_t+\tilde{s}_{i,t})\ell_{i,t}
    +D_t+T_t
    \geq
    \sum_{i=1}^{N}
        p_{i,t}\gamma_{i,t}c_{i,t}
    +\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell_{i,t+1}.
\end{align}
Here $w_t$ is the nominal wage, $m_t$ is nominal money holdings, and
$\tilde{s}_{i,t}$ is the realized premium on borrowing. It is defined
by
\begin{align}\label{eq:hh_realized_premia}
    &(1+r_t+\tilde{s}_{i,t})\ell_{i,t}
    \notag\\
    &\qquad=
    \min\Bigg\{
        (1+r_t+s_{i,t})\ell_{i,t},
        \notag\\
    &\qquad\qquad
        (1-\tau_{i,t})p_{i,t}\gamma_{i,t} y^d_{i,t}
        +
        p_{K,t}(1-\xi_{i,t})
        \left[
            (1-\delta^K)k_{i,t}+i_{i,t}
        \right]
    \Bigg\}.
\end{align}
The variable $p_{K,t}$ is the price of physical capital, $D_t:= \sum_{i=1}^N d_{i,t}$ is
total nominal profit from firms, and $T_t$ is a lump-sum government
transfer. Total nominal consumption expenditure is
\begin{align*}
    P_tC_t
    :=
    \sum_{i=1}^{N}
        p_{i,t}\gamma_{i,t}c_{i,t},
\end{align*}
where $P_t$ is the aggregate price index.

\subsubsection{Government}
There is a government that conducts fiscal and monetary policy. On
the fiscal side, the government collects taxes or provides subsidies
to firms and rebates the resulting revenue to households as a
lump-sum transfer:
\begin{align}\label{eq:government_transfer}
    T_t
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^{N}
        \tau_{i,t}p_{i,t}\gamma_{i,t}y^d_{i,t}.
\end{align}

On the monetary side, we follow \cite{laoOptimalMonetaryPolicy2022} to impose the cash-in-advance constraint 
\begin{align}\label{eq:hh_cia_constraint}
    M_t \geq P_tC_t,
\end{align}
where the total money supply or nominal aggregate demand is directly set by the monetary authority, and the risk-free nominal interest rate $r_t$ is determined by the money market clearing condition.
